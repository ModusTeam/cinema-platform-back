# Checkout and payment decoupling — Phase 1

## Current sequence and Phase 1 API

Before this phase, `POST /api/orders` validated seat locks, called `OrderReservationService`, committed a `Pending` order and `Reserved` tickets in PostgreSQL, then called `OrderCheckoutOrchestrator`. It returned the checkout result, so a decline could still leave a persisted order. The orchestrator claims `Pending -> PaymentProcessing` with a conditional PostgreSQL update before calling loyalty or `IPaymentService`. Confirmation persists `Paid` and `Valid` together; a definitive decline persists `Failed` and `Released`; an ambiguous result retains `Reserved` and becomes `PaymentOutcomeUnknown`. No transaction spans the provider call. The ticket partial unique index and the claim also protect expiration and pending cancellation races.

Phase 1 adds `POST /api/orders/reservations` with `{ sessionId, seatIds }`. It uses the same lock validation and reservation service and returns `{ id, totalAmount, status: "Pending" }`. It does not call payment, GOLD, or loyalty. `POST /api/orders/{id}/checkout` accepts `{ paymentToken, useLoyaltyPoints, applyGoldUpgrade }` and returns the existing order ID result. `GET /api/orders/{id}/status` returns `{ id, status }` for its owner. Checkout loads the persisted order and tickets, checks ownership and lifecycle eligibility, and uses the existing atomic claim and outcome handling. The old `POST /api/orders` body and reserve-then-checkout behavior remain available and share these services. `GET /api/orders` still returns order history.

Clients may move to: lock seats, create a reservation, store its ID, start checkout once, then read status after a timeout. A paid retry returns the order ID. Processing, unknown, failed, expired, and cancelled orders reject another charge; an uncertain result requires reconciliation, not a new checkout request. The new reservation route does not provide request idempotency: retrying creation after a lost response may create a second order or hit seat uniqueness. Coordinate frontend migration before changing the old POST; no frontend code was changed. The mock payment provider and current response conventions are retained.

## Ownership and provider boundary

Orders owns creation, seat reservation, ticket price snapshots, totals, `Pending`/`PaymentProcessing`/`Paid` lifecycle, confirmation, expiration, and cancellation decisions. Checkout applies the existing GOLD and loyalty rules while Orders remains the coordinator. Future Payments owns durable payment and refund attempt IDs, provider idempotency keys, provider calls and webhooks, result lookup, and reconciliation. Provider IDs and credentials must stay outside Orders.Domain. `Order.PaymentTransactionId` currently records the successful provider transaction for confirmation and refund; it cannot identify an in-flight attempt.

`IPaymentService.ProcessPaymentAsync(amount, currency, paymentToken, ct)` and `PaymentResult` are the existing provider-neutral boundary. `PaymentResult` carries success with a transaction ID, a definitive failure code, or the current `payment_outcome_unknown` code. The contract is sufficient to keep payment execution out of order creation, but insufficient for production recovery. A future Payments contract must accept a durable attempt identity and provider idempotency key, return a provider-neutral attempt/outcome identity, and support querying a definitive outcome by that identity. Persist the attempt under Payments ownership before dispatch; correlate it with order ID and checkout request ID. A refund needs its own attempt identity and lookup. Order status alone is neither provider idempotency nor proof of payment. No PaymentAttempt entity is added to Orders.Domain or to the database in Phase 1.

The current PostgreSQL claim guarantees at most one concurrent caller of a Pending order starts a charge in this application path. It does not distinguish a crash just before the provider call from one after the provider received it. The mock provider does not offer authoritative lookup or idempotency. Do not automatically retry `PaymentProcessing` or `PaymentOutcomeUnknown`. Reconciliation must ask the real provider through Payments and then apply a definitive paid or failed transition with the existing tickets. A successful charge followed by failed local confirmation likewise requires reconciliation. Duplicate reservation requests remain a separate API idempotency problem.

## GOLD, loyalty, and refunds

Reservation-only creation saves base ticket prices and has no loyalty side effects. At checkout, GOLD eligibility and discount are calculated from the persisted session and reserved ticket snapshots. `UseGoldUpgradeAsync` consumes the upgrade before payment; the order discount and upgraded ticket are saved before points deduction. A retry after precharge reset sees `IsGoldUpgraded` and does not consume a second upgrade. Loyalty points are calculated and deducted before payment using the deterministic order refund/deduction keys; `PointsUsed` is recorded when purchase is confirmed. A definitive payment decline attempts point refund and GOLD rollback. An uncertain result retains the reservation and upgrade for reconciliation. A confirmation failure can leave external GOLD/points effects and a discounted order without a confirmed purchase. The persisted ticket upgrade and price snapshot, order total, points, and successful transaction ID are the available reconciliation evidence; an attempt record is still needed. No second deduction path was introduced.

Paid cancellation currently refunds payment, then points and GOLD, then saves `Cancelled` and `Refunded` tickets. A refund or loyalty success followed by a later failure can diverge from the database; concurrent paid cancellations can duplicate external refunds. Phase 1 does not redesign this workflow. Payments must own refund attempts and reconciliation; Orders must decide whether cancellation is eligible and apply the final lifecycle change.

## Domain events and durable delivery

`DispatchDomainEventsInterceptor` runs before EF saves, clears events, and publishes both direct domain events and wrapped notifications. `OrderPaidIntegrationEventHandler` sends `TicketPurchasedMessage` and `NestJsTicketPurchasedEvent` to RabbitMQ before persistence. `OrderPaidEventHandler` unlocks Redis and sends SignalR completion. Failed and cancelled handlers unlock Redis; cancellation also sends SignalR seat notifications. These handlers load tickets from EF's tracked state before save. `OrderCheckoutOrchestrator` separately publishes `TicketPurchasedEvent` after the paid save. The current consumers of the RabbitMQ messages perform external work. None of these external effects is atomic with the order transaction, and an event cleared before a failed save is lost.

Phase 2 should keep internal domain processing that needs pre-save tracked entities at the appropriate in-process point, but construct immutable integration-event payloads from the final state and store them in an EF outbox row in the same PostgreSQL transaction as the business change. Use MassTransit's EF Core transactional outbox with the existing `ApplicationDbContext` and PostgreSQL migration if it meets the direct publish and endpoint-send needs; verify both messages and direct purchase event are covered. Dispatch after commit with retries and consumer idempotency. Redis/SignalR notifications need a deliberate post-commit retry policy. Do not blindly move all domain handlers after save or claim exactly-once delivery. Outbox delivery is at least once and consumers must deduplicate. No outbox or schema change is made in Phase 1.

## Phase 2 and Payments handoff

Phase 2: implement the approved outbox split, test save failure and broker outage, ensure purchase messages use final paid state, define consumer deduplication, and close GOLD/points confirmation and cancellation compensation gaps with controlled failure tests. Keep the existing claim and expiration guards from #53. Confirm the direct `TicketPurchasedEvent` publication path and all pre-save integration handlers before changing dispatch order.

Future Cinema.Payments: persist charge/refund attempt identity, add provider idempotency and outcome lookup, handle webhooks, reconcile processing/unknown orders and interrupted refunds, and provide a definitive outcome to Orders. A real provider is required to verify those guarantees. Phase 1 can only prevent a second charge through the current order claim while the same order remains processing or unknown; it cannot establish what happened remotely after a crash.

The original stabilization findings D04-D07 and R01/R02 remain the risk baseline. #53 reduced R02 with the atomic claim and protected reservations, and reduced D05 by moving the direct purchase publish after the paid save. D04 pre-save external effects, D05 lost/duplicate publication, D06 GOLD compensation, D07 refund consistency, and R01 provider uncertainty still require the work above.
